Fit: The distribution the values were found consistent with, and its p-value, or "No clear fit" when every family is rejected. It does not change as you move through the list below.
SF: The Shapiro-Francia normality statistic W' on up to 5,000 values, from 0 to 1, where 1 indicates perfect normality. The p-value is Royston's approximation: how likely a W' this low is if the values were normal.
Skew: Measures asymmetry of the data distribution (positive = right-tailed, negative = left-tailed).
Kurt: Tail heaviness compared to normal distribution (high = heavy tails, low = light tails).
Median: Middle value when data is sorted.
Mean: Average value of all data points.
Std: Standard deviation (spread of data around the mean).
CV: Coefficient of variation (std/mean, relative variability independent of scale).

Q-Q Plot:

Compares your data against the distribution chosen in the list, with the parameters fitted to it. Points following the diagonal reference line indicate a good match. Deviations show where your data differs from the theoretical model.

Histogram:

Shows the frequency distribution of your data as bars, with a theoretical distribution overlaid as a line in the theme's secondary series color. The height of bars represents how many data points fall in each bin range. Compare bar heights to the theoretical line to see how well your data matches the expected distribution.


Distributions:

Each family with its p-value, highest first: a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test calibrated by 199 samples drawn from the fit and refitted. "<0.005" when none came close. "n/a" when the family cannot describe the values at all (a log-normal of negative values); choosing one says why. Choosing a family compares the values with it; it does not claim they fit.

Scale:

The last row of the list names the histogram's scale; s toggles it. Log needs positive values: asked for on others, the histogram stays linear and the scale reads Linear in the warning color.
